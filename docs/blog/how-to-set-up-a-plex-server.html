Set up a Plex server, own your media.

By the Kuvacult team · Published November 2, 2026

Streaming made it effortless to watch almost anything, and just as effortless to lose it. Films leave services without warning, “purchases” turn out to be licences, and the version you loved can be quietly edited. A home media server puts the collection you own back in your hands. Here’s why it’s worth doing, and how to set one up with Plex.

Why owning your media still matters

Catalogues change constantly. Streaming libraries are built on licensing deals that expire, so a film that’s on a service this month can be gone the next. If it’s the one you were planning for Friday, that’s the end of the plan.

Digital “purchases” are usually licences. Buying a film on most storefronts gives you the right to stream it for as long as the store keeps that right. When a licence changes hands, titles can disappear from libraries people paid for.

The cut you see isn’t guaranteed. Services sometimes swap in altered versions, trim scenes or replace music when rights lapse. A disc on your shelf is the film exactly as it was released.

Quality and extras. A Blu-ray typically carries a higher bitrate than a stream, and it often comes with commentaries, deleted scenes and documentaries that never make it to a streaming page.

No DRM, no black screens. Streaming services block screen sharing, which is why a Discord or séance stream of Netflix comes out black. Files you own play anywhere, including in a shared screen with friends.

What you’ll need

Very little. Any computer that can stay switched on will do, whether that’s an old laptop, a desktop or a network storage box (NAS). Add storage to match your ambitions: a feature film runs from a few gigabytes to around 30 for a full-quality Blu-ray copy, so an external drive of a few terabytes holds a substantial collection.

Then the software. Plex Media Server is free and runs on Windows, macOS, Linux and most NAS devices. If you’d prefer fully open-source software, Jellyfin does a similar job.

Where your collection comes from

The point of a home server is to keep media you legitimately own. That might mean DRM-free digital purchases, your own home videos, public domain classics, or copies of discs you’ve bought. The rules on copying discs vary by country, so check what’s permitted where you live before you digitise your shelf. Plex is a library for your collection, not a way to download films you haven’t paid for.

Setting up Plex, step by step

1

Download Plex Media Server for your computer or NAS, install it and sign in with a free Plex account.

2

Make a Movies folder and give each film its own subfolder named Title (Year), for example The Thing (1982). This is what lets Plex fetch the right poster, synopsis and cast.

3

In Plex, add a library, choose Movies and point it at that folder. Plex scans it and builds your catalogue in a few minutes.

4

Install the Plex app on your TV, phone or console, or open it in a browser, and your collection is ready to play anywhere in the house.

Watching away from home

Playback on your home network is free. Streaming your library remotely, or sharing it with friends who watch from their own homes, now needs a paid plan: either the server owner has a Plex Pass, or each viewer gets a Remote Watch Pass. Check Plex’s current pricing before you rely on it.

Questions

Is Plex free?

Plex Media Server and local playback on your home network are free. Remote streaming of your personal media requires a Plex Pass for the server owner or a Remote Watch Pass for the viewer.

What do I need to run a Plex server?

A computer or NAS that can stay switched on, enough storage for your films, and the free Plex Media Server software.

How should I name my movie files for Plex?

Put each film in its own folder named Title (Year), with the file named the same way. Plex uses that to match the correct poster and details.

Does Kuvacult work with Plex?

Yes, alongside it. Plex plays the film and Kuvacult handles the shared watchlist, the pick and the séance. Share Plex playback in a séance and friends can watch without a Plex account or remote pass.

Can I share Netflix or Disney+ in a Kuvacult séance?

No. Streaming services use DRM that blocks screen capture. Séances work with films you own, such as those in your Plex library.

Is it legal to put my movies on Plex?

Plex is legal software for organising media you own. Laws on copying discs differ by country, so check the rules where you live.
